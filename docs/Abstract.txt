Title: De novo computational design of epitope-specific nanobodies against the Orthobunyavirus oropoucheense nucleocapsid for point-of-care diagnostics

The 2023-2025 Oropouche outbreak, with over 23,000 cases across the American continent, exposed critical public health gaps. Although RT-qPCR remains the diagnostic gold standard, it requires specialized infrastructure inaccessible in endemic rural areas. Coupled with reinfection rates approaching 60%, these gaps underscore the urgent need for low-cost point-of-care (POC) alternatives such as lateral flow assays. Traditional nanobody discovery relies on animal immunization, which is slow and unable to target predefined epitopes, and no epitope-directed POC reagents have been reported for the Orthobunyavirus oropoucheense species complex.
We developed a multi-stage in silico workflow to design epitope-specific nanobodies against the nucleocapsid (N) protein of this species complex. Nucleocapsid sequences spanning OROV and closely related strains were curated via MMSeqs2 and modeled with AlphaFold2/ColabFold. Linear and discontinuous epitopes were predicted using DTU Bioinformatic Services and filtered by strict sequence conservation and minimal RNA-binding interference. Validated VHH scaffolds (PDB: 8QF4, 9EZU) underwent forced docking with HADDOCK v2.4, followed by de novo CDR redesign using RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, and AlphaFold-Multimer. Candidates were progressively filtered through structural metrics, PDBePISA interfacial ranking, and 100 ns all-atom molecular dynamics (GROMACS 2023, AMBER99SB-ILDN, TIP3P).
Two pan-specific epitopes were identified: Epitope B (residues 75–85) and Epitope C (residues 171–186), both highly conserved across the Oropouche complex with zero cross-reactivity against Zika, Dengue, Chikungunya, and Mayaro viruses (BLASTp, E-value >1.0 against epitopes). From 96 designs, 14 passed computational filters. Epitope C candidates satisfied strict thresholds (pLDDT ≥ 0.75, pTM ≥ 0.70, PAE ≤ 10 Å, RMSD ≤ 2.5 Å); the top complex achieved pLDDT of 0.863, pTM of 0.793, and interface RMSD of 1.03 Å. Epitope B designs exhibited greater flexibility and were retained under relaxed criteria (best: pLDDT 0.78, pTM 0.57, RMSD 4.2 Å). PDBePISA identified 4 hydrogen bonds and 1 salt bridge for Epitope B, and 3 hydrogen bonds and 3 salt bridges for Epitope C. Subsequent 100 ns MD simulations sustained an average of 7.66 (Epitope B) and 7.67 (Epitope C) intermolecular hydrogen bonds per frame; although backbone RMSD reached 7.98 and 8.32 Å reflecting conformational sampling, the persistent interfacial H-bond network indicates the binding interface remained engaged throughout both trajectories.
This workflow provides a generalizable, epitope-directed framework for rapid, low-cost nanobody design against emerging arboviruses, supporting the accelerated development of species-specific POC lateral flow assays for resource-limited settings in Latin America.


Key words: De novo protein design, Nanobodies, Orthobunyavirus oropoucheense, Point-of-care diagnostics, Molecular dynamics
